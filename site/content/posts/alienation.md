I think I speak for a lot of folks in tech when I say all of _this_ **gestures vaguely at everything** feels really, profoundly weird. AI coding is certainly the biggest technological change I've experienced in my career; having joined the industry right about the time cloud computing was kicking off I never spent a significant amount of time working in on-premises systems.

On the one hand the tools are incredibly exciting, even exhilarating. But at the same time _work_ feels way more stressful, uncertain, and ... something else. I've been trying to think of the way to express it and I think the Marxist theory of _alienation_ comes closest. Marx said that capitalist production _alienates_ workers from a) the products of their labour, b) the activity of production itself, c) from what he called their "species-being" (sort of like the essence of being human), and d) from other people. AI feels like it certainly does all of that. But also sometimes it doesn't!

The thing that I am finding super weird about AI is that it is both incredibly anti-alienating in the personal context and incredibly alienating in the corporate/capitalist context. So I find myself oscillating, sometimes within minutes, from feeling super excited about this tech and what I can do with it, to feeling stressed, burnt out, alienated by its implications in the workplace.

This is a lot less clear-cut when it comes to art or to writing, but at least in the context of coding, tinkering, _building_ things, AI tools are a massive accelerant for individuals who want to own their own stuff. Without AI tools, with the time I have as a mid-career parent of school-aged children, this site would not exist, and this post, had I written it, would live on a platform where I own neither it nor the streams of data it might generate.
