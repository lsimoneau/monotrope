## Jasmine Sun — "Claude Code Psychosis" (2026)

### Moves observed

1. **Extended analogy as structural spine.** The parkour vision analogy opens the piece, names the core problem (perception, not capability), and recurs throughout. The analogy *is* the thesis. She doesn't explain abstractly then illustrate. The illustration does the arguing.

2. **Self-deprecation as credibility move.** Catalogues her fumbles in detail (wrong model, permission prompts, lost files) before making big claims. Not false modesty — it buys authority. By the time she says "this is clearly autonomous," you trust her because she's already proven she isn't a hype merchant.

3. **Tonal pivots without signposting.** Moves from personal narrative to cultural commentary to structural argument without announcing the shift. Transitions happen through rhythm changes, not topic sentences.

4. **Comic detail as commitment.** "I added Tahoma, Comic Sans, and Wingdings to the PDF font selector." Specificity proves she did the thing and carries personality without stating it. The detail *is* the voice.

5. **Counterargument embedded in narrative, not as a section.** The turn ("most of my problems are *not* software-shaped") emerges from her own experience. She doesn't set up a debate. She tells you what happened and the counterargument falls out of the story.

6. **Ending with texture, not a bow.** Returns to the scene ("My Terminal icon is jumping up and down on my taskbar") instead of restating the thesis. Ends in the middle of the experience, not above it.

7. **Coining compounds that carry arguments.** "Scalemaxxed sterility," "Claudecrastination" — portmanteaus and coinages that compress a whole critical position into a phrase. The vocabulary does the arguing so the sentences don't have to.

8. **Register-mixing within a clause.** "Scalemaxxed sterility replaced with bespoke builds and pizzazz" — internet culture, clinical, craft/fashion, mid-century showbiz in one clause. Each word pulls from a different lexicon. No two adjacent surprises come from the same direction. This is what makes the prose feel like riffing rather than writing in a single voice. (Compare: Stephenson's Snow Crash opening does the same move stretched across sentences — "brain-drained" / "historical inequities" / "smeared them out" / "Pakistani brickmaker".)

### Drill idea (for a future session)

**Register-mixing as an antidote to committee language.** Take a flat sentence from a draft and pull its word choices in different directions — no two unusual words from the same register. The move is the opposite of what committee language does (flatten everything into one register). Worth drilling against current tic of committee language ("value-add", "good for my context and productivity").
